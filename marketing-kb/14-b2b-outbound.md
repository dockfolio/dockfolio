# 14 — B2B Outbound

**When to use:** When your ICP is businesses (not consumers), your ACV is ≥ $50/mo (unit economics support sales effort), and you can't wait for SEO/content to compound. When the first 10 customers will come from cold outreach, not from a viral Product Hunt launch. When you've read `02-pre-traction.md` and decided direct reach is the right channel.

**Primary sources:** Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler, *Predictable Revenue* (2011) — the book that defined modern SaaS outbound. Patrick Dang's cold email frameworks. Lemlist's and Reply.io's public playbooks. Steli Efti (close.com) on outbound for early-stage. Jason Bay's outbound coaching content.

## The Predictable Revenue core insight

Before *Predictable Revenue*, "outbound sales" meant generalist salespeople who did prospecting, qualification, closing, and onboarding all at once. Aaron Ross's insight while at Salesforce: **specialize the roles.** Separate "people who find and qualify leads" (SDRs) from "people who close them" (AEs) from "people who keep them" (CSMs). For a solo founder this sounds irrelevant — you're all three roles at once — but the principle still matters: **do one role per time-block**, not all three simultaneously.

For indie SaaS the practical translation: **Mondays are prospecting mornings. Tuesdays are first-touch. Wednesdays are follow-up. Thursdays are demos/closing. Fridays are onboarding and retention.** Batch by role, not by customer. Context-switching between "find new prospects" and "close existing ones" in the same hour is the #1 killer of outbound productivity.

## Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) — not "target market"

"Target market" is a marketing term. "ICP" is an operations term. The difference matters because ICP has to be specific enough to BUILD A LIST from. "Small businesses" is not an ICP. "Direct-to-consumer e-commerce brands on Shopify with $500K-$5M annual revenue, 5-20 employees, based in German-speaking countries" IS an ICP. Every word in that definition is a filter you can apply to Apollo, Crunchbase, or Shopify's public app review data.

The five ICP dimensions:
1. **Firmographics:** industry, size (employees, revenue), geography, tech stack
2. **Trigger events:** recent funding, recent hiring, recent tool switch, recent leadership change
3. **Pain signals:** public complaints, negative reviews of a competitor, job postings for roles your product replaces
4. **Decision maker role:** who has budget authority? Who is the *champion* (internal advocate)?
5. **Disqualifiers:** who should you NEVER sell to? Enterprise? Government? Nonprofits with no budget? Be explicit.

If you can't write a 5-line ICP and then go to Apollo and pull 500 matching companies, your ICP is too vague. Narrow until you can.

## The 10/5/1 list rule

A cold outreach sprint is only as good as the list. For each batch:
- **10 sources.** Don't buy one big list. Combine: LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo, Crunchbase, public Shopify App Store reviews, GitHub stars, podcast guest lists, conference attendee lists, Twitter followers of category leaders, Substack commenters in your niche, job board postings mentioning specific tools.
- **5 data points per prospect.** Name, company, role, email, one "unique-to-them" fact (their recent LinkedIn post, their latest blog, the tool they just reviewed, the conference they spoke at). The fifth data point is what makes the email NOT look like mass spam.
- **1 reason each person is on the list.** Write it next to their row. If you can't name the reason in 10 words, cut them.

A list of 50 prospects with 5 data points each beats a list of 5,000 with only email + company. Always.

## The cold email anatomy (6 elements, in order)

Every cold email that gets a reply has these six elements, in this order:

1. **Subject line** — 3-6 words, lowercase, no "Hi [Name]", no "Quick question." Best pattern: a specific noun phrase that signals relevance ("Shopify shops in Berlin" or "your Trustpilot reply"). Open rate target: 45-60%.

2. **Opener** — one sentence about THEM, not about you. Reference their specific thing. "Saw your reply on the Shopify forum about migrating product videos — same thing drove me up the wall last year." If you can't write an opener about them specifically, you haven't done enough research on this prospect.

3. **Relevance bridge** — one sentence explaining why you're writing specifically to them. "I'm building [product] because I saw 12 shops with the exact issue you described."

4. **Value proposition** — one sentence. What outcome could they get? Not features. "Most shops cut product-video production time from 4 hours to 90 seconds."

5. **Social proof or credibility** — one line. "Three Shopify shops in your category are already using it." Or "I ran [previous relevant thing]." Or "Gary from [Shop they know] recommended I reach out." Skip if you have nothing real — don't fake it.

6. **Call to action** — ONE specific, low-commitment ask. Not "let me know if interested." Not "can we hop on a 30-minute call?" The highest-converting ask is a direct yes/no: "Worth 10 minutes on Thursday at 2 PM your time?" Or "Can I send a 60-second Loom showing how this would work for your shop?" Specificity + low commitment = the math of reply rates.

The whole email: 60-90 words. If it's longer, cut. If it's shorter, you probably skipped elements 1-3.

## The sequence (3-7 touches over 14-21 days)

One email is not outreach. Outreach is a sequence. Standard B2B cold sequence:

- **Day 0:** Email 1 (above)
- **Day 3:** LinkedIn connection request with no note (silent signal you exist)
- **Day 5:** Email 2 — short reply to email 1 with "bump" and one new angle. Max 3 sentences.
- **Day 8:** LinkedIn DM — reference their content, softer tone, no pitch
- **Day 12:** Email 3 — "breakup email." "Haven't heard back, totally understand — I'll stop reaching out. If the timing is just wrong, no hard feelings; reach out when ready." The breakup email gets the highest reply rate of any email in the sequence (often 5-10%) because people feel the obligation to respond to a "final" message.
- **Day 18 (optional):** Voice note or video message (Loom) — reserved for high-value prospects
- **Day 21:** Hard stop. Move them to "not now" and re-sequence in 90 days.

**Rule:** never send more than 7 emails in a sequence. After 7 you're harassing, not prospecting. The reply rate on email 8+ is indistinguishable from noise.

## Reply rate benchmarks (be honest with yourself)

Industry averages for B2B cold outbound:
- **Open rate:** 30-50% (if your domain isn't burned)
- **Reply rate (positive OR negative):** 3-8% on a cold list; 10-15% on a warm/personalized list
- **Positive reply rate (actually want to talk):** 1-3% cold; 4-8% warm
- **Booked meeting rate:** 0.5-2% of sends
- **Close rate from booked meetings:** 15-30% for SMB, 5-15% for mid-market

**Math:** to close 1 customer/week, you need ~3-5 meetings/week, which needs ~150-300 sends/week, which needs ~300-600 prospects on your list at the start of the week. This is why B2B outbound is brutal — the funnel is real.

If your reply rate is below 3%, the problem is ALMOST ALWAYS the list (bad ICP match), not the copy. Fix the list first. If it's still below 3% after fixing the list, then fix the copy.

## Personalization: real vs fake

**Fake personalization:** "Hi [First Name], I saw you work at [Company]."
**Real personalization:** "Hi Sarah, I watched your 2-minute product video on the Shopify Store page yesterday — the way you explain the stitching detail is exactly the kind of thing most DTC brands skip."

Fake personalization is a merge field. It's WORSE than no personalization because the recipient knows it was automated. Real personalization requires 2-3 minutes of actual research per prospect and shows up as a specific sentence that couldn't have been written about anyone else.

**Rule:** if your personalization could be auto-generated with templates, it isn't personalization. Real personalization takes 2-5 min per prospect. Budget your outreach around 20-30 prospects per hour, not 200.

## The "referral, not interest" qualifier

Aaron Ross's best qualifying move: instead of asking "are you interested?", ask "is this a priority right now, or should I reach out to someone else on your team?"

This does two things:
1. Disqualifies non-buyers fast (they'll say "reach out to X") — you get a warmer lead from the same contact
2. Flags the real priority — if they say "not a priority right now," that's a HARD no, which is 10x more useful than a polite "interesting, let me think about it"

Never let prospects sit in a "maybe" state. Force a yes, a no, or a referral. Three outcomes, not two.

## CRM discipline for solo founders

You don't need Salesforce. A Google Sheet with these columns is enough:
| name | company | email | list source | reason on list | last touch | next touch | status |

"Status" is one of: `new`, `touched`, `replied`, `meeting`, `negotiation`, `closed-won`, `closed-lost`, `not-now`.

Two rules:
1. **Every prospect has exactly one status.** No "hot lead / warm lead" labels. States, not adjectives.
2. **Every status change is dated.** You need to see "this prospect has been in negotiation for 45 days" to trigger a re-engagement move or a loss-marker.

One 10-minute CRM hygiene pass per day keeps the sheet alive. Skip it for a week and the system dies.

## Common failure modes

1. **Pitching instead of qualifying.** You're not trying to convince them — you're trying to find out if they're a fit. If they're not a fit, move on fast.
2. **Writing an essay.** 200-word emails get read less than 70-word emails. Every word fights for space.
3. **Only sending Monday-Friday 9-5.** Test Tuesday 6 AM, Saturday 10 AM, Sunday 8 PM. A human is a human, not an office hours schedule.
4. **Never following up.** 70% of positive replies come on touch 3-5, not touch 1. If you give up after the first email, you're leaving 70% of the value on the table.
5. **Buying lists instead of building them.** Bought lists are burned. Build your own, always. 10x the reply rate.
6. **No sender reputation hygiene.** Burning your main domain's reputation by blasting cold emails from it. Set up a separate `outbound.yourcompany.com` subdomain with its own SPF/DKIM/DMARC. Warm it up for 2 weeks before sending volume.

## When outbound is the wrong channel

Outbound doesn't work for:
- **Consumer products.** Consumers don't respond to cold B2B-style emails. Different game.
- **Sub-$20/mo products.** Unit economics can't support the human hours. Needs self-serve.
- **Hyper-commoditized markets.** If your prospect gets 50 cold emails a day for the same category, yours drowns.
- **Products with a long internal sales cycle.** Enterprise with 6-month procurement cycles — outbound from a solo founder doesn't survive the long game.

If any of these apply, the answer is SEO, content, community, or product-led growth — not outbound. See `04-content-and-seo.md`, `15-plg-motions.md`, `16-category-design.md`.
